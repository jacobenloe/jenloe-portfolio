Physics lab

Measuring liquid surface tension

Surface tension is the force at the surface of a liquid. I measured it with a laser, with Brooks Kioschos, in a paper dated May 1, 2025. The setup used a helium-neon laser, wavelength 632.8 nm. Standing waves on the surface of the liquid made a diffraction pattern. The pattern gave the surface tension. We tested water, water with soap, and mineral spirits, at frequencies from 80 to 560 Hz. The screen was 342.2 cm from the liquid. The full procedure, data, and uncertainty analysis are in the original report. I still need to dig out that report. I cannot say more about the results until I have it in front of me.

Lab build. December 2023.

Amplitude modulation and demodulation

Amplitude modulation, or AM, is a way to carry an audio wave on a carrier wave. I built a device that does it, with Price Pruett, in a lab report dated December 15, 2023. The device takes an audio wave, the messenger wave, and modulates it onto a carrier wave from 0.5 to 3 kHz. Then it demodulates the signal to recover the audio. The goal was a modulation index of 1, which is a perfect modulation. The circuit, the measurements, and how close we got to that goal are in the original report.

Review paper. December 2023.

Ultrasonic testing of lithium-ion batteries

In this paper, I will review the current research on ultrasonic testing, or UT, in lithium-ion batteries. UT is a non-destructive testing method. A transducer sends an ultrasonic wave into the battery. The wave reflects at each interface between different materials. Time of flight, or TOF, is the time from the initial pulse to the first reflected echo. TOF tracks the state of health of a battery. State of health is the ratio of the current maximum capacity to the initial capacity. As a battery degrades, TOF increases. UT can show metal defects, such as dendrites, and it can detect electrical abuse. It cannot yet be shown to work for all battery sizes and shapes, and it has not been proven to prevent electric vehicle fires. That is a limit of the current research, not a failure of the method. College project

Compressed air wobbler engine

A wobbler engine, as I use the term here, is a small engine that runs on compressed air. The cylinder rocks, or wobbles, as the piston moves, and that rocking opens and closes the air ports. I built one as a college project. I machined every part by hand, part by part, fitting each one until the engine ran.

Work-order management system

A work-order system, as I use the term here, is a way to track maintenance jobs so none of them get missed. I built one at The Connection at CU Boulder. It schedules preventive maintenance by lane frame counts. A lane frame count is a measure of how much use a lane has had, so the system uses that count to decide when maintenance is due. It is still in use.
